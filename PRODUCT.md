# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Freelancers — designers, developers, writers, consultants — who send scope documents to clients. They currently manage approvals over email threads, which makes it hard to track what was agreed and when.

**Their context:** Between project work and client communication. They reach for Worklit when they need a deliverable approved, not when they want to write another email.

**The job:** Define deliverables with prices, send a magic link to the client, get per-item approval or rejection, and have a signed record of what was agreed. The alternative is email chaos — lost threads, "I thought that was included," and disputes with no paper trail.

## Product Purpose

Worklit replaces email-based scope negotiation with a structured approval flow. A freelancer creates a project, adds scope items, sends a link, and the client approves or rejects each item. Approved items auto-populate an invoice. Every decision is logged.

**Success (current stage):** Get the first real users on the app. As a first product with no existing user base, validation means freelancers choosing Worklit over email for real client work.

**Core differentiator:** Ease of use. Not a CRM, not project management — just scope, approval, record.

## Brand Personality

**Calm authority.** Like a well-drafted contract, not a flashy SaaS dashboard. Confident, clear, professional without being cold. Minimal without being sparse.

**Three words:** Confident. Clear. Editorial.

**Tone in copy:** Direct, not clever. Freelancer POV ("you," not "your team"). Acknowledge the pain before offering the solution. Short sentences. No exclamation marks in body copy.

## Anti-references

- No electric/neon accents or aggressive gradients
- No startup purple
- No dense feature grids or enterprise dashboard density
- No red/orange for rejected states (rejected is neutral, not punitive)
- No "AI slop" aesthetics — avoid Inter/Roboto, avoid purple-on-white gradients, avoid cards for everything

## Design Principles

1. **Every element earns its place.** Nothing decorative survives. If removing something doesn't change the experience, it shouldn't be there.

2. **Green means one thing: approval.** The green accent is the only meaningful color signal. Everything else is warm stone. No secondary accent colors, no color-coded status rainbows.

3. **Freelancer-first, not enterprise.** The interface serves one person and their clients. No team features, no org hierarchies, no permission systems. Simple enough that a client needs no account to use it.

4. **The record is the product.** Every approval, rejection, and change is timestamped and stored. The changelog isn't a feature — it's the peace of mind that prevents disputes.

5. **Unseen details compound.** Spacing, type rhythm, motion curves, focus states — none are individually noticed, but together they create trust. The interface should feel like a well-set contract, not a web form.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- WCAG 2.1 AA for all interactive surfaces
- `prefers-reduced-motion` support for animations
- Touch-friendly tap targets on mobile (especially the client review page — clients open these on phones)
- Color is never the sole indicator of state (status badges always include text labels)
